Key numbers

219calls booked by prospects in the US and Canada (all timezones) in the last 90 days. That is just over half (52%) of all 419 calls booked.
38%of all US and Canada calls are booked for 7pm to 10pm Eastern time. That is 4pm to 7pm in California and midnight to 3am UK. This is the busiest time, and only George works it. Since 14 Oct he starts at 8pm Eastern, so 7pm is no longer offered.
0slots offered between 2pm and 8pm Eastern time, on any day. That is 11am to 5pm in California and 7pm to 1am UK. No sales rep works those hours.

These numbers cover every US and Canada prospect, wherever they live. Times are given in Eastern time (New York) so everyone is measured on the same clock.

US prospects like to book late morning to lunchtime, or in the evening. During a normal US working afternoon we offer nothing at all. For someone in California, that gap is 11am to 5pm their time, the middle of their working day.

What someone in the US sees when they book

Imagine a fresh week with nothing booked yet. These are the times a US prospect would be offered on the booking page, in their own local time (GHL converts the times automatically). Pick where they live below.

How to read this: each time is when a 30-minute call can start. "8pm - 11:30pm" means they can start a call at 8:00, 8:30, 9:00 and so on, up to 11:30. Prospects pick a time, not a person. GHL then gives the call to one of the sales reps working at that time, taking turns between them. So when two or three reps overlap, they share those calls.

What we suggest

  1. Ask George about 7pm. Check whether he meant to stop working 7pm to 8pm Eastern time (midnight to 1am UK). That hour had 12 US calls booked in the last 90 days, all on Friday and Saturday evenings.
  2. Try opening the US afternoon. Cover 7pm to 1am UK time on 2 or 3 evenings a week for a month, and see how quickly those slots get booked.
  3. Set Patrick's hours. He is on every booking calendar, but because he has no working hours set, the booking page never offers him. He has had only 2 calls in 90 days.
  4. Rely less on George. He takes 65% of US calls, so on any day he is off, most US evening slots disappear.
  5. Let prospects book further ahead. Right now they can only see 5 days ahead, and when we checked there were only 81 free slots in that whole time.
How we worked this out We count someone as a US or Canada prospect from the timezone on their GHL contact, and checked it against their phone number (US and Canada numbers start with +1). The two agree for almost everyone. The country field says "GB" for everyone, so it cannot be used. "Calls booked" includes calls that happened, no-shows, upcoming calls and cancellations. Deleted bookings are not counted. All times are summer time. UK clocks go back on 25 Oct and US clocks on 1 Nov, so for that one week the UK times shown here are an hour out. Free slots were checked on 15 Oct. All of our consultation booking calendars showed the same slots.
